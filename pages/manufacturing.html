What it's built from

6061 Aluminum — Primary Body

Aluminum 6061 was selected for its combination of corrosion resistance, ease of machining, and sufficient structural integrity at operating pressures. A single 24″ × 24″ × 36″ block forms the nozzle body.

Plexiglass Side Walls

Transparent walls allow schlieren imaging, shadowgraph photography, and laser diagnostics for flow visualization without requiring window ports in the aluminum body. Estimated cost ~$200.

Fasteners & Gaskets

Stainless fasteners and elastomeric gaskets seal the assembly and prevent helium leakage at block interfaces and side wall joints. Estimated cost ~$75.

Fabrication

Machining
process

The contoured flow path demands tight tolerances and smooth surface finish. CNC milling is the critical step.

Critical tolerance: The nozzle contour must match the MOC profile precisely. Deviations generate Mach waves that degrade flow uniformity at the test section. Surface finish of Ra < 1.6 μm is required throughout the supersonic portion.

CNC Contour Milling

The MOC-derived wall profile is converted into a CNC toolpath and milled into the aluminum block halves. Rate: $60–120/hr, estimated total ~$1,500.

In-House Cutting & Drilling

Flat aluminum plates are cut to size and fastener holes drilled using Stevens' machine shop — no external vendor, minimal cost.

Assembly & Sealing

The two nozzle halves are bolted together with gaskets at the parting line. Plexiglass side walls are fitted and sealed before installation into the shock tunnel.

Tunnel Integration

Fitting the
existing tunnel

The new Mach 14 nozzle fits within the existing Stevens shock tunnel dimensions without structural modifications. This constraint was central to the geometry selection — the planar design was partly chosen because it can be sized to fit the current test section.

The primary operational change is switching the working fluid from nitrogen to helium. Helium supply and regulator systems must be verified for compatibility, but no major plumbing changes are needed.

Structural mods to tunnelNone required
Working fluid changeN₂ → He
Nozzle mountingExisting flanges
Optical accessPlexiglass side walls
Previous Mach capabilityMach 6
New Mach capabilityMach 14

Helium molecules are smaller than nitrogen — standard seals may require upgrade to PTFE or Viton materials to prevent leakage at operating pressures up to 10 MPa.
