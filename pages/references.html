Background

Key concepts

—

Method of Characteristics

A numerical technique for solving hyperbolic PDEs by tracking characteristic lines. In nozzle design, it produces wall contours that cancel internal Mach waves and deliver uniform exit flow.

—

k-ω SST Turbulence

The Shear Stress Transport model blends k-ε in the freestream with k-ω near walls, providing accurate boundary layer prediction in adverse pressure gradients common in diverging nozzle flows.

—

Prandtl-Meyer Expansion

Describes isentropic supersonic flow turning around a convex corner. The Prandtl-Meyer function ν(M) is the foundation of the MOC nozzle design procedure for both 2D and axisymmetric geometries.

—

Shock Tunnels

A shock tunnel uses a reflected normal shock to generate high total enthalpy flow for short durations (milliseconds). The Stevens tunnel drives the nozzle with high-pressure driver gas — originally N₂, now He.

—

Helium Wind Tunnels

Helium's high speed of sound and extremely low liquefaction temperature make it ideal for high-Mach ground testing. The Ames M-50 is a canonical example of a large-scale helium facility (Ref. 02).

—

Optical Flow Visualization

Schlieren and shadowgraph methods exploit density gradients to image shocks, expansion fans, and boundary layers. The plexiglass side walls enable non-intrusive optical diagnostics (Ref. 06).

Hypersonic Nozzle Project

Senior design project at Stevens Institute of Technology. Extending hypersonic ground test capability to Mach 14 with a helium-driven shock tunnel nozzle.
