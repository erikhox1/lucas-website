About the Project

From Mach 6
to Mach 14

The Stevens shock tunnel currently generates flows up to Mach 6. This project redesigns the nozzle section to reach Mach 14 — a regime relevant to high-speed boundary-layer physics, Hypersonic flight, and re-entry vehicles.

The key enabler is helium. Its uniquely low liquefaction temperature allows the tunnel to operate at the low temperatures required for Mach 14, something that is not achievable with air or nitrogen at any practical facility scale.

Design Pillars

How it comes together

01

Method of Characteristics

A Python-based MOC solver designs the diverging wall contour by tracing characteristic lines through the supersonic flow field and canceling internal Mach wave reflections.

02

CFD Validation

RANS simulations with k-ω SST turbulence modeling capture viscous boundary layers, flow separation, and shock structure that the inviscid MOC approach cannot predict.

03

Precision Machining

The contour is CNC-milled into 6061 aluminum, with plexiglass side walls for optical access. The assembly fits the existing shock tunnel with no structural modifications required.

Nozzle Design

Gradual expansion
for uniform flow

The diverging section is shaped by the Method of Characteristics to produce a smooth, gradual expansion — eliminating internal shocks and delivering the uniform, low-turbulence flow field essential for accurate model testing.

Rather than a simple conical expansion, the MOC-derived contour carefully sets the wall turning angle at every axial station, producing parallel, shock-free flow at the exit plane without disturbing the test model.

Research Applications

What Mach 14
makes possible

—

High-Speed Boundary-Layer Physics

At Mach 14, boundary-layer transition and shock–boundary-layer interactions enter regimes inaccessible at lower speeds. This facility enables direct measurement of transition onset, separation, and reattachment at flight-relevant Reynolds numbers.

—

Re-Entry Vehicles

Peak aerothermal loads during orbital re-entry occur in the Mach 10–18 range — directly accessible with this facility upgrade and critical for heat shield and control authority design.

—

Hypersonic Flight

Sustained hypersonic flight at Mach 10–20 demands ground-test data on aerothermal loads, wave drag, and stability at conditions that cannot be replicated in lower-speed facilities.
