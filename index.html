About the Project

From Mach 6
to Mach 14

The Stevens shock tunnel currently generates flows up to Mach 6. This project redesigns the nozzle section to reach Mach 14 — a regime relevant to scramjet propulsion, re-entry vehicles, and hypersonic glide vehicles.

The key enabler is helium. Its uniquely low liquefaction temperature allows the tunnel to operate at the high stagnation temperatures required for Mach 14, something that is not achievable with air or nitrogen at any practical facility scale.

"Design and fabricate a shockless Mach 14 nozzle for the Stevens shock tunnel using helium as the working fluid."

Design Pillars

How it comes together

01

Method of Characteristics

A Python-based MOC solver designs the diverging wall contour by tracing characteristic lines through the supersonic flow field and canceling internal Mach wave reflections.

02

CFD Validation

RANS simulations with k-ω SST turbulence modeling capture viscous boundary layers, flow separation, and shock structure that the inviscid MOC approach cannot predict.

03

Precision Machining

The contour is CNC-milled into 6061 aluminum, with plexiglass side walls for optical access. The assembly fits the existing shock tunnel with no structural modifications required.

Research Applications

What Mach 14
makes possible

—

Scramjet Systems

Supersonic combustion ramjets require Mach 5+ inlet conditions. Mach 14 enables testing of dual-mode ramjet/scramjet transitions and fuel injection schemes at flight-relevant conditions.

—

Re-Entry Vehicles

Peak aerothermal loads during orbital re-entry occur in the Mach 10–18 range — directly accessible with this facility upgrade and critical for heat shield and control authority design.

—

Hypersonic Glide Vehicles

Boost-glide systems cruise at Mach 10–20. Ground testing at these speeds is essential for understanding aerothermal loads and shock–boundary-layer interactions.
